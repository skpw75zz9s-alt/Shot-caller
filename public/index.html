    The line · how safe the lead is

    How it worksThe lead is how far price is past the target. The move left is how far price usually still travels in the time remaining (price × its 1-minute volatility × √minutes). Their ratio is the cushion: under 0.25 is a razor-thin coin-flip zone, around 1 is solid, 3+ is near-certain. "Rounds actually finished" comes from every round in the last three weeks of 1-minute candles, at the same cushion and time left: history, not the model. In the last 60 seconds of a crypto round Kalshi settles on the average of the final 60 one-second prints, so each second that passes is banked: the settlement watch shows where that average lands if price stays put, and what the seconds left would have to average to flip it. Pressure is buyers vs sellers on the exchanges; the record shows how often the leading side still won when they disagreed. Sessions: when the big markets' trading days open and close, which is when volume and volatility change.

        Reading the dollar, yields and the rest of its market…

        Actual moveFrom its driversIts ownVolume (amber: 3× usual)

          How it worksGold, silver and oil are traded as futures, so their price is set by people buying and selling, but most of that trading reacts to a few things. Macro: the US dollar (they're priced in dollars, so a stronger dollar usually pushes them down), the 10-year Treasury yield (higher rates make gold, which pays nothing, less attractive) and stocks. Its market: the rest of its own market (silver, platinum and copper for gold; Brent, gasoline, diesel and natural gas for US crude), minus what the macro already explains, so a move they share is sector-wide buying. Its own: whatever's left, its own buyers and sellers and news (for US crude: inventories, US supply, big orders). Fitted on the last 3 days of 1-minute moves; over any window the parts add up to the actual move. Volume underneath shows when trading was heavy (Yahoo's futures volume). It explains moves, it doesn't predict them, and a market moving together isn't proof one caused the other.

          Reading every market's last few days…

            How it worksEvery market is read next to the others that move with it: Bitcoin drags the other coins, crypto often follows the Nasdaq and S&P 500 in US hours, gold and oil move on the same news. ρ is how closely each one has moved with this market minute by minute over the last few days (1 = in lockstep), β how much this market has moved per 1% of it, and catch-up is how far this market has lagged what the others just did (β × their 5-minute move − this market's). A model of the next 5 minutes from all of them is refitted every 30 minutes and scored on data it never trained on against "no change"; it only nudges the bot's odds (±5 points at most) once it proves itself.

                            How it works

                            Cone: where BTC is likely to close (inner band 50% of outcomes, outer 90%), centred on the expected path and recalibrated from its own record. The expected path (dashed white) runs through the cone; the shaded zone right of the dotted line is the forecast, and the round's close is marked. Drag across the chart: the legend reads every candle, and over the forecast it reads that minute of the cone. Every forecast source (off by default, Chart tools): where each source says BTC closes: Bot (the bot's own odds), Kalshi (the close its price implies), Exchanges (the trend across every streamed market, the global ones included), Engine, Guide, Micro and Move model. Each line's moves are scaled to how far they've really carried, and the lines are blended by their error covariance into the expected path (the cone's centre); all of it is graded every minute. Flip watch: the path carries today's momentum and turns where a flip looks ripe; ↺ marks each predicted turn, checked afterwards. Bull and bear heads mark the bot's UP and DOWN calls; small ones mark EMA 9/21 crosses. The faded animal behind the candles is the trend (bull while EMA 9 is above EMA 21). Live orders: orders that pushed the price at least $5 and 30% of a typical 10-second move bubble up at their price, green for buyers and red for sellers (gold for a whale). B / S / ✕ flags mark your buys, sells and bail-outs.

                            Chart patterns
                            Reading the chart…
                              How it worksThe bot reads the chart for the shapes traders look for: head and shoulders (and inverse), triangles (ascending, descending, symmetric), rising and falling wedges, channels, flags, support and resistance breaks after 3+ touches, and candlestick patterns (hammer, shooting star, engulfing, morning and evening star, three white soldiers and black crows, piercing line and dark cloud cover). It never peeks ahead: a swing high or low only counts once 3 more candles have closed, and a pattern counts on the candle that confirms it (a close through its neckline or line). Each kind is tested on the server's last 3 weeks of 1-minute candles like every indicator: picked on the older rounds, checked on the newest ones it never saw. Only a kind that proves itself there (z 3+) touches the calls; until then it's shown here, on the chart and on the signal board, and nothing more. Patterns are a popular idea, not a guarantee: many of them do no better than a coin flip on 1-minute crypto charts, and the record here says which.

                                    How Shot Caller calls a round

                                    1. The question. Each Kalshi 15-minute BTC market asks one thing: will BTC finish above the target (UP / YES) or below it (DOWN / NO)? Kalshi settles on the average of an index built from several exchanges over the final minute.
                                    2. The bot's odds. From where BTC is, how far it is from the target, how much time is left and how wild BTC has been, the bot works out the chance each side wins. It takes the most cautious of three volatility guesses, so its confidence leans low on purpose.
                                    3. The gap. A side is only worth buying when Kalshi's price is below the bot's odds by enough to beat Kalshi's fee. That's "buy the low".
                                    4. Hold odds and flip risk. Confidence moves as BTC moves. The bot simulates the rest of the round 300 ways and counts how often confidence stays above 80 to the end: that's hold odds. Flip risk is the rest. In testing, predicted hold odds came true within a few points.
                                    5. The Profit level (official). The bot only calls sides Kalshi prices at 60-90¢, where a win pays at least 10¢ on the dollar, and only when even its most cautious odds beat Kalshi's price plus the fee by 8¢ or more, from 12 to 5 minutes left. Confidence has to hold for a minute and hold odds be 80%+. Once called, the side is locked for the round; it switches once at most, at half size, on clear evidence of a flip.
                                    6. The 10-minute pick. Every round at 10:00 left the bot locks a side, using its own odds combined with Kalshi's price (half and half at first, then in whatever mix its graded picks show is most accurate). It's marked BUY only when Kalshi's price is in the 60-90¢ band and the worst case still beats it after the fee; otherwise it's a lean, not a buy.
                                    7. Learning. The server watches every round, around the clock: how wild each half hour of the week usually is, whether its odds came true, and the gap between Coinbase and Kalshi's index.

                                    The chart

                                    • EMA 9 / 21: fast and slow trend lines; the fast one above the slow one means the short-term trend is up. The chart shows it as a big faded bull (up) or bear (down) behind the candles, with a small bull or bear where the lines cross.
                                    • RMA: a smoother, slower average (the kind RSI uses).
                                    • Bollinger 20 ±2σ: a band around the 20-bar average; touches of the edges mean a stretched move.
                                    • VWAP: the average price traded since the round opened, weighted by volume.
                                    • Floor / ceiling: the round's low and high so far.
                                    • Forecast cone: where BTC usually ends by the close at the bot's volatility. If the target is outside the outer cone, the round is close to decided.
                                    • RSI 14: momentum, 0-100; above 70 is stretched up, below 30 stretched down. MACD: trend momentum; bars above zero mean it's building up.

                                    Tug of war and whales

                                    Every exchange trade has an aggressor: a buyer who lifted the ask or a seller who hit the bid. Buy pressure (the bull) and sell pressure (the bear) are each side's share of the dollar volume over the last 2 minutes, and the side that's winning steps up and pulses; round flow is the total since the open. The tug of war counts every trade on Coinbase, Kraken, Bitstamp, Gemini and Binance.US, and the Live orders tape shows them as they happen, next to the trades on the Kalshi contract itself (who's buying UP and DOWN, and at what price). Whales are single trades above your minimum (Alerts). These are context only: they don't change the call.

                                    Honest limits

                                    No call is certain. Even 98% calls lose about 1 time in 50, and calls that safe are priced high on Kalshi, so a loss costs much more than a win pays. Check the Record tab: it grades every call against Kalshi's real result.

                                    Loss lab · why it lost

                                    After every loss the bot looks back at what it saw and what happened next, and says why it most likely lost. It keeps score of those reasons over every graded call. A reason the record proves (30+ calls, clear by z 2.8, in both the older and the newer half) changes the calls from then on: the round call takes the other side, and calls worth buying skip that kind. Nothing makes 15-minute rounds certain; this fixes the mistakes the record proves, it can't remove luck.

                                              How it works

                                              From "The BTC Trader's Playbook": its 15-minute liquidity-sweep strategy, run exactly as written and measured the way it says to (100+ examples, win rate, average reward-to-risk, max drawdown, fees). With the 1-hour trend, a 15-minute candle sweeps yesterday's high or low and closes back inside; a market structure shift on above-average volume confirms; entry on the retest; stop beyond the sweep plus a volatility allowance; target 2R. Its other secrets (sweeps, structure shifts, fair value gaps, order blocks, volume divergence, VWAP, open interest, funding) are inputs the micro model learns from: they sway the candles and calls only once proven.
